# Strata art pipeline

Both pilots from `strata_outline.md` §4.3 are done; P1 is now the creature
sprite pipeline every species goes through. Everything is generated by
scripts in `tools/strata/`. Run them from the repo root, and commit the outputs.

## P2: era palettes for battle backgrounds and the grass overlay

**What it does.** In a deep-time map, the battle background and the tall-grass /
long-grass / shaking-grass / ripple / ash field effects now use the same colour
transform as that era's overworld tilesets. Without this they kept Hoenn colours.

| Era | Transform | Maps |
|---|---|---|
| `ERA_IMPACT_WINTER` | `impact_winter` | Levee/River/Backswamp (winter) |
| `ERA_BIGHORN_PETM` | `petm` | Red Beds |
| `ERA_WHITE_RIVER` | `oligocene` | White River |
| `ERA_TRAP_CAVE` | `ice_age` | Steppe, Trap Cave |
| all other eras | stock | |

**Data.** `python3 tools/strata/gen_tilesets.py` writes the tileset variants (as
before) and also:

- `graphics/battle_environment/strata/<transform>/<env>.pal`: the 9 outdoor
  environments (tall grass, long grass, sand, underwater, water, pond, rock, cave,
  plain). Colour 0 of each 16-colour bank is kept.
- `graphics/field_effects/palettes/strata/general_1_<transform>.pal`: the shared
  `FLDEFF_PAL_TAG_GENERAL_1` sprite palette.
- `src/data/strata_era_art.h`: the tables. The era→transform map is
  `ERA_TRANSFORMS` and the environment list is `BATTLE_ENVS`, both in `gen_tilesets.py`.

**Engine.** `src/strata_art.c` looks up the current map in `sStrataMapEras`
(`src/data/strata_map_eras.h`, generated by `gen_maps.py`). It uses the map
and not `VAR_STRATA_ERA`, so the art always matches the tileset on screen. The hooks:

- `battle_bg.c`: both battle-background palette loads go through
  `Strata_GetBattleEnvironmentPalette()`.
- `field_effect.c`: the three field-effect palette loaders go through
  `Strata_GetFieldEffectPalette()`. So does the grass palette of the
  overworld-encounter spawn animation (`wild_encounter_ow.c`). This swaps only
  `GENERAL_1`.

**Adding an era** (e.g. after A1 adds a `paleocene` transform): add the transform
to `TRANSFORMS`, map the era in `ERA_TRANSFORMS`, rerun `gen_tilesets.py`. No C changes.

**Preview.** `python3 tools/strata/gen_tilesets.py --preview DIR` writes
`battle_bg_<env>.png` and `tall_grass_overlay.png`. Each shows stock first, then
each transform.

**Limits.**
- `GENERAL_1` also colours ripples, ash and hot-spring water, so they shift too.
  That is the intended result: they sit on the same ground.
- A sprite palette stays loaded until the overworld resets sprite palettes, which
  happens on a warp. A *map connection* between maps of different eras would keep
  the old grass colour until the next warp. Today every era change is a rig warp.
- The transforms are global colour maps. The impact-winter battle BG is almost grey,
  matching the tileset. New background *art* (badland cliffs, ash) is still task A6/§4.1.
- Stadium, building and legendary environments are untouched.

## P1: creature sprites (`tools/strata/sprites/`, `gen_sprite.py`)

**Rule: every creature is original art.** No stock sprite, overworld sprite or
cry ships in the final game. Until a species has its own art, `gen_species.py`
copies the `borrow=` stock sprites as a placeholder; that count must reach zero
(see *Borrowed art*, below).

### Commands

```bash
python3 tools/strata/gen_sprite.py mesodma knightia      # species (art folder names)
python3 tools/strata/gen_sprite.py --plan small_mammal   # every spec of one body plan
python3 tools/strata/gen_sprite.py --all                 # every species with a spec (also: no args)
#   add --preview DIR for review images (Windows: /mnt/c/Users/lukem/AppData/Local/Temp/<you>_png)
python3 tools/strata/gen_sprite.py --contact OUT.png [--scale 2]   # all 87 front sprites on one sheet
python3 tools/strata/gen_sprite.py --borrowed [--strict]  # art status per plan; --strict exits 1 if any borrowed
python3 tools/strata/gen_sprite.py --plans                # taxonomy table and suggested artist lanes
python3 tools/strata/gen_species.py [--strict-art]        # then make
```

### Package layout

| Path | Contents | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| `sprites/core/geom.py` | shapes (`Ellipse`, `Tube`, `Loft`, `Union`, `Poly`), `Part`, `Model`, `turn`, `curve` | lead |
| `sprites/core/raster.py` | coverage (4×4 supersampling), dome shading, cast shadows, contours, outline; `Canvas` painters (`line`, `disc`, `polyline`, `shade`, `texture`) | lead |
| `sprites/core/color.py` | `ramp()`, `Palette` (index 0 transparent, ≤ 15 colours), pigment variant, CIELAB icon-palette fit | lead |
| `sprites/core/image.py` | crop/place, 4bpp/1bpp indexed PNG and JASC writers | lead |
| `sprites/core/build.py` | all outputs of one species, overworld sizing, `CUSTOM` | lead |
| `sprites/core/preview.py` | per-species previews, contact sheet | lead |
| `sprites/core/plan.py` | the `BodyPlan` interface (read its docstring) | lead |
| `sprites/taxonomy.py` | **the one table** species → body plan, plus suggested lanes | lead |
| `sprites/check.py` | art status (`--borrowed`) | lead |
| `sprites/plans/<plan>.py` | the parametric template for one body plan | that plan's artist |
| `sprites/specs/<plan>.py` | `SPECS`: the species data (proportions, ramps, features) | that plan's artist |

A body plan is a template, `side(p, phase, step)` → `Model`, plus optional
`overworld(p, view, step)` and `back(p, phase)`. A spec is a dict merged over the
plan's `DEFAULTS`. Common spec keys: `ramps`, `front`/`back`/`icon` render scales,
`shiny` (per-ramp `deg`/`sat`/`val`), optional `ow` (`size`, `length`, `scale`,
`end_zoom`, `shadow`, `tracks`), `footprint`, `model` (a one-off replacement for the
plan's side view). Everything else is plan-specific and documented in the plan
module. `plans/fish.py` (Knightia) and `plans/small_mammal.py` (Mesodma) are the
working templates; the other 18 plans are registered stubs whose docstrings list
their species and the anatomy to get right.

Regenerating the two pilots after the refactor gives byte-identical battle
sprites, palettes, icons and footprints; `CUSTOM` gained the `@overworld` line and
the folders gained `overworld.png`.

### Outputs (`graphics/pokemon/strata/<folder>/`)

| File | Format |
|---|---|
| `anim_front.png` | 64×128 (two 64×64 frames), 4bpp indexed |
| `back.png` | 64×64, same palette |
| `normal.pal`, `shiny.pal` | JASC, 16 colours, index 0 transparent |
| `icon.png` | 32×64 (two frames), indices into one of the 6 shared icon palettes (CIELAB best fit; the outline maps to the icon dark grey) |
| `footprint.png` | 16×16 1bpp (blank for fish) |
| `overworld.png` | 6 frames in a row: south, south step, north, north step, west, west step (east = west mirrored by the engine). 32×32 each (192×32), or 64×64 (384×64) for animals over 1 t. Same palette as the battle sprites |
| `CUSTOM` | marker; presentation overrides (`frontPicSize`, `frontPicYOffset`, `backPicSize`, `backPicYOffset`, `iconPalIndex`) and `@overworld SIZE_32x32 SHADOW_SIZE_M TRACKS_FOOT <views>` |

**gen_species.py integration.** With `CUSTOM`, `gen_species.py` keeps the folder,
replaces the borrowed presentation fields with the `CUSTOM` lines, and, if
`overworld.png` exists, emits `gObjectEventPic_<Sym>` (`INCGFX_COMP`, `-mwidth 4
-mheight 4` or 8/8), the overworld palettes (the battle `normal.pal`/`shiny.pal`),
a `sPicTable_<Sym>` in `strata_pokemon.h` and an `OVERWORLD(...)` block with
`sAnimTable_Following` in `strata_families.h`. `SpeciesToGraphicsInfo` then finds
real data, so every `OBJ_EVENT_GFX_SPECIES(<SPECIES>)` map object (bosses, herds,
set pieces in `tools/strata/maps/`) shows the creature. Species without an
overworld sheet still get the engine's substitute placeholder (itself stock art:
another reason to reach zero). Animation, shadow and cry are still borrowed.

### Borrowed art (strict mode)

- `gen_sprite.py --borrowed` prints, per plan, own vs borrowed, stub vs template,
  specs without output, and overworld sheets that are side-only. Today: **85 of 87
  borrowed** (own: Knightia, Mesodma). `--strict` exits 1 while any remain.
- `strata_families.h` states the count in its header comment.
- `gen_species.py --strict-art` (one run) or `ART_STRICT = True` (permanent) makes
  every borrowed species an `#error` in `strata_families.h` and fails the script.
  Flip `ART_STRICT` to True the day the count reaches 0; `gen_species.py` prints a
  reminder then. From then on a borrowed sprite breaks the build.
- `test/strata_art.c` checks that every Strata species with an overworld sheet
  wires it up (pic table, `sAnimTable_Following`, dynamic palette, both palettes).

### Cries (still borrowed; to do)

All 87 cries are copied from the `borrow=` species. Proposal for an original
solution: `tools/strata/gen_cry.py`, a procedural synthesiser writing the format the
cries already use (8-bit mono 10512 Hz WAV, `sound/direct_sound_samples/cries/
strata_<name>.wav`), with a `CRY` marker that `gen_species.py` respects the way it
respects `CUSTOM`. One vocal model per body plan, scaled by body size:

- **Mammals:** source–filter. A glottal pulse train with F0 falling with mass
  (roughly F0 ∝ mass^-0.4: squeaks for Mesodma, grunts and bellows for Bison),
  formants from vocal-tract length (∝ skull length), and a contour per plan:
  yip/bark/howl (canids), growl/roar (cats, bears), bleat (ungulates), whinny
  (equids), trumpet + rumble (mammoth), chitter (rodents, primates).
- **Birds:** a two-voice syrinx: two independent FM sinusoids with trills;
  gobbles and booms for galliforms (Centrocercus air-sac pops), croaks for cranes.
- **Archosaurs and turtles:** closed-mouth booming (low-frequency, as in crocodiles
  and ostriches; the cautious reconstruction for dinosaurs, Riede et al. 2016),
  hisses as filtered noise, jaw claps. Hadrosaur crest resonance is a low formant.
- **Fish and rays:** swim-bladder drumming pulses (living drums/croakers) and splashes.
- **Bats:** an FM sweep pitched down into the audible range.

Each cry is a few parameters in the species spec (`cry=dict(...)`), so it can live
in the same spec files. Not implemented yet.

### How a sprite is made

1. Shapes are in model units (sprite pixels at scale 1), centred on the origin,
   head to the left. A plan builds a list of `Part`s (shape, ramp, z order, dome
   flatness, optional per-point `region` ramp for countershading, outline colours).
2. Coverage: 4×4 supersampling per pixel; the nearest part covering half a pixel owns it.
3. Shading: each part is a dome lit from the upper left, quantised into its ramp.
   Nearer parts cast a 1-px shadow onto farther ones.
4. Contours: a nearer part draws its `inner` colour where it overlaps a farther part.
5. `details` paint eyes, mouths, fin rays, scutes, fur texture, toes.
6. A 1-px outer outline in each part's `outline` colour; `post` draws over it (whiskers).

The back sprite is the side model mirrored at the `back` scale unless the plan has
a `back()` view. The icon is the model at about 0.5 scale; painters drop fine
features when `cv.big` is False (scale < 0.8). The overworld west frames are the
side model at a scale that gives the mass-class body length (20 px under 1 kg, 24
under 30 kg, 27 under 300 kg, 30 under 1 t, 44/56 px in 64×64 frames above); the
south/north frames come from the plan's `overworld()` at 1.25× that (`end_zoom`).

**Previews** (`--preview DIR`): `<name>_sheet.png` (front frames, back, shiny front
and back, icons, at 1× and 3×), `<name>_front_x6.png`, `<name>_battle.png` (foe and
player positions on the tall-grass BG, stock and impact-winter), `<name>_ow.png`
(the 6 overworld frames at 1× and 4×, and the creature beside the player on grass).

### Style guide (the quality bar for artist agents)

**Views.**
- *Front (foe) sprite:* side profile facing left, with 3/4 cues: far-side limbs
  set back and up and darkened (`light=-0.25`), near limbs overlapping the body with
  contour lines, the far ear/horn peeking over the head. The pilots showed profiles
  read well at 1×, and the profile is where the science is (snout length, posture,
  toe count, tail). A full 3/4 body is not attempted: rotating 2D parts does not
  convince. Where identity is frontal (ceratopsian frill and horns, bighorn curls,
  uintathere knobs, the forward-facing eyes of primates and cats), turn the *head*
  up to ~30° towards the viewer while the body stays in profile.
- *Back (player) sprite:* minimum is the mirrored profile at 1.05–1.1× the front
  scale. Where the dorsum carries identity (turtle carapace, ankylosaur armour, ray
  disc, wings), write a `back()` view from above and behind.
- *Overworld:* south faces the camera (head, ears, forelimbs in front of the body),
  north shows the back and tail, west is the profile. Chibi proportions are fine
  (heads up to ~1.2×). Bottom pixel row 30 (62 in 64×64 frames).

**Size.** Battle sprites fill 48–64 px of width whatever the real size (a mouse
and a bison both read); giants also fill the height. Let `place()` measure; never
exceed 64×64. Overworld lengths come from mass (above).

**Palette ramps.** ≤ 15 colours plus transparent. Typical split: outline (1), main
coat 4 shades, secondary (belly, skin) 3, tertiary (horn, beak, fin) 2–3, accents
1–2. Shades step evenly in value (about 15–25%), lights slightly warmer and
shadows slightly cooler; the surface facing the viewer lands on the
second-lightest shade. The outline is a very dark version of the main hue, never
pure black. Avoid fully saturated colours. Index 0 is transparent only.

**Outlines.** One-pixel outer outline everywhere, in the outline colour; pale
parts that would look heavy (fins, membranes, feathers' edges) outline in their own
darkest shade. Inner contours only where a nearer part overlaps a farther one
(head over body, near leg over belly), in the nearer part's darkest shade. No
anti-aliasing against the transparent background (battle backgrounds vary).
No single stray pixels; texture (fur dashes, scales, osteoderms, feathers) is a
sparse regular pattern that stays off the outline and the lightest shade.

**Animation.** Frame 2 is a small biological action (sniff, gape, tail beat, crest
raise, wing lift), not a squash. Walk step 1 swings the limbs about the hips.

**Judge at 1× and 3×.** At 1× (`_sheet.png` top row, `_battle.png`): the silhouette
names the animal; the eye is visible; it stands out on both battle palettes; it is
distinct from the other species of its plan (check the contact sheet). At 3×:
clean clusters, even ramps, unbroken outline, no jaggies on long curves, details
placed on the anatomy. Check the icon (`_sheet.png`) and the overworld mock
(`_ow.png`): the south frame must read as the same animal.

**Science.** Colour and soft tissue are reconstructions and must look plausible,
not fantastical: use living relatives and ecology (countershading; agouti browns
for small mammals; spots/stripes for forest dwellers, plain coats in open country;
known colours for living species such as pronghorn, bighorn, black-footed ferret,
turkey, sage-grouse). Feathered dromaeosaurs and oviraptorosaurs, lipped
theropods, no shrink-wrapped skulls. Note every speculative choice (fur colour,
crests, ear shape) in the spec's comment so the Codex can say so. The shiny palette
is a natural morph (leucistic, melanistic, erythristic, a paler or darker
population), not neon.

### Adding the next creature

1. Find its plan with `gen_sprite.py --plans`. If the plan is a stub, start the
   template from the closest working one (`small_mammal` for quadrupeds, `fish` for
   lofted bodies) and keep shared geometry parametric.
2. Add the spec to `specs/<plan>.py` with the real proportions; note evidence and
   speculation in its comment.
3. Iterate with `--preview` until it passes the style guide at 1× and 3×.
4. Run `gen_species.py`, then `make`. `git diff strata_families.h` should show only
   the 5 presentation lines and the new `OVERWORLD` block for that species.
5. Run `--contact` and compare it with its plan-mates.

Expect 30–60 minutes per species for a variant of a working template, and a few
hours for a new template.

### Lessons and limits

- **What works:** silhouettes, consistent lighting and outline style, honest
  colour, exact format compliance, instant re-renders when the science changes.
- **Global lighting needs tuning per ramp length**: the face-on surface should land
  on the second-lightest shade, or bodies go flat.
- **Painted details carry the appeal** (eye, mouth line, head/body contour, fin
  rays). They are hand-placed in model coordinates, so each species still needs an
  artist's eye.
- **Limits:** profiles only (see Views); the two-frame animation is a small pose
  change; no hand-pixel cleanup pass; complex anatomy (frills, feathers, armour,
  shells, wing membranes) needs new primitives or textures in its plan. Icons are
  limited by the 6 shared icon palettes: browns fit well, strong blues and reds
  poorly. Overworld end-on views are simple and benefit most from per-plan care.

### Lessons: carn lane (canid + carnivoran)

- **One proportion-driven engine for 17 quadrupeds.** `plans/canid.py` builds the
  whole animal from ~40 numbers (body length/depths, leg height and digitigrady,
  head cranium/muzzle/jaw, ear, tail) instead of hand-placed ellipses;
  `plans/carnivoran.py` reuses `side`/`overworld` with different specs (cats,
  bear, weasels, creodonts). New quadrupeds (ungulates, hyenas) could start here.
- **Primitives worth promoting to core** (all in `plans/canid.py`): `Facet`
  (a convex polygon with a ridge, so ears, sabres and claws shade like low
  pyramids instead of flat plates), `Turned` (any shape rotated about a pivot:
  posing a whole head or jaw without rebuilding it), `Path` (a Tube that knows
  the fraction along it: tail tips, rings), `paint(cv, shape, colour, owner)`
  (fill a shape restricted to one part's pixels: inner ears), and colour
  `marks` (first-match rules per part group: ellipses, half-planes, spot
  lattices, stripes, tail rings/tips, socks) evaluated in head-local
  coordinates so they follow the head when it turns.
- **Muzzles are tapered tubes**, not ellipses: an ellipse muzzle reads as a
  duck bill. The lower jaw is its own part just behind the head (z below), so
  the head's contour line *is* the mouth line, and the gape frame is a jaw
  rotation about the hinge plus a skin-coloured triangle in the gap.
- **End-on overworld views in head units.** Sizing the south/north views from
  the side proportions gave tall leg-columns with tiny heads. Instead size
  everything in U = cranium radius × ~1.9 (×2.4 for tiny long-bodied species),
  shorten the legs, put the face (eyes 1×2 px, a 2-px nose, a pale muzzle oval)
  over the chest and the ears big; north = rump, hind legs, hanging tail with
  its tip mark, the back of the head and ears.
- **Lineage look by shared marks, not shared colours:** pale lips/throat/chest,
  dark ear tips, a tear streak and a dark tail tip on every dog-line species,
  with forest spots/rings fading out generation by generation (Protictis →
  Miacis → Hesperocyon → Leptocyon), reads as one family even across very
  different palettes (coyote, swift fox, dire wolf, Epicyon).
- **Scale per spec by trial:** long tails make side views wide and flat; curl
  the tail up and raise the head before shrinking `front`. `place()` errors give
  the exact overflow (scale × 62/size fixes it in one step).
